Option 1 · quantified screening bound

Move the complete knee bolt pair

Move both axes across the post depth toward the unloaded edge. Keep the pair’s vertical spacing. This increases the effective depth used by the equation.

What it could address: the post’s reduced-depth allowance.
Still required: adjoining spine geometry and strength, grain-end distance, other holes, washer lands, access, and the changed force distribution. Improving the post can worsen the spine.
Option 2 · no accepted dimensions

Revise the adjoining block arrangement

Develop the post connection and its adjoining solid-timber block together, so a useful bolt location has supported timber on both sides of the interface.

What it could address: the partner constraints that may prevent a simple bolt move.
Still required: complete joint geometry, support and load transfer. The dashed outline is a design question, not a proposed stock size or qualified capacity.
Option 3 · load-path hypothesis

Transfer some load through bearing

Investigate a supported timber bearing interface that carries part of the relevant load through compression, while bolts retain their necessary duties.

What it could address: demand on the vulnerable connection, if the revised load path demonstrates it.
Still required: contact direction and reversals, bearing area, any cut effects, splitting and compatible sharing. A bearing seat does not automatically remove the post’s existing section shear.

Why review the test before changing the frame?

The two knee bolts transfer about 151 N in the relevant direction. The 985 N is total internal post shear between them, including other connections and floor reactions. The saved force balance reproduces that total, but the correct application of the connection provision must be established; substituting the smaller bolt total would also need justification.

The recorded near-end equation is Vr = (2/3) F′v b de (de/d)². Its cubic dependence on effective depth explains why bolt location matters so much. “Effective depth” is an equation parameter, not a physical notch or a strip of wood that has been removed.

Best next sequence: confirm the demand/application → screen a coordinated post-and-block layout → check both timber sides and the changed load path → integrate only a supported revision.
