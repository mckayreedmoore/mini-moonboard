# Reference analysis

## Sources

- User-supplied photograph of a completed freestanding Mini MoonBoard.
- Moon Climbing, [The Best Boulders on the Mini MoonBoard](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wDB6Lg4x_lM),
  especially 0:52-1:09.

The photograph and video frames are not redistributed here. They are visual
references without a reliable scale, so their apparent proportions must not
be used as fabrication dimensions.

## Visible construction sequence

The time-lapse between 0:52 and 1:09 appears to show:

1. a ladder-like panel-support frame assembled flat;
2. broad plywood side members and cross-members added while accessible;
3. the frame raised into an A-frame stance;
4. diagonal rear legs and transverse bracing establishing the footprint;
5. the lower and then upper climbing panels attached to the face frame; and
6. a wide cap member tying the structure together at the top.

This is an assembly-order reference only. Cuts, joints, fastener types,
fastener counts, member thicknesses, and load paths are not visible well enough
to reproduce safely.

## Completed-frame observations

The supplied photograph shows a self-supporting structure with:

- a two-by-two array of main climbing panels and a separate kicker;
- four support lines behind the panel field, consistent with Moon Climbing's
  four-support requirement;
- broad, shaped plywood side members with rounded floor feet;
- rear legs forming an A-frame profile;
- through-fastener heads visible at several side-member connections;
- a full-width top cap; and
- an open landing area below the overhang for crash pads.

The side members look compatible with laminated sheet construction, but the
image does not establish their material grade, layer count, adhesive, internal
joinery, or structural capacity.

## Adopted and rejected inferences

Adopted for the design brief:

- indoor freestanding A-frame;
- plywood-forward construction language;
- access for assembling major pieces flat before raising them; and
- a taller custom kicker that keeps the active kicker clear of the crash pad.

Not adopted without further evidence:

- member dimensions estimated from pixels;
- the photographed bolt pattern as an engineered connection schedule;
- an assumption that no hidden blocking, plates, or anchors exist; or
- an assumption that the reference structure satisfies local requirements.
