# Leg and weight ideas after the 2x12 trial

These are unmodeled concepts, not changes to the selected braced frame or a new
planning commitment. Every concept must meet the retained loads and check the
actual joints, stability and support reactions. The previous 0.674 braced result
and 2.079 unbraced result favor retaining an effective triangular load path.
They do not establish numerical success probabilities for a new arrangement.

| Priority | Concept | Why it could work | Decisive constraint |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One continuous unnotched 2x6 knee per side | Keeps the mechanism that relieved the upper joint; removes the lap and its eight bolts | Existing rim and leg attachment faces occupy different planes. Align them or provide a checked offset connection; do not just delete the splice |
| 2 | Broad plywood side gusset across leg and rim | Carries moment through a broader fastening region with a clean side profile | Plywood shear/buckling, fastener groups, perpendicular-grain loading, access and actual attachment planes |
| 3 | Full triangular side frame with a floor rail | Can transfer more load as member axial forces and restrain foot spread | Adding a floor rail does not turn the current upper bolt group into a pin. The actual upper and lower connection behavior, floor reactions and footprint must be checked |
| 4 | Rated steel strap/rod bracing | Potentially removes bulky timber knees while retaining triangulation | Tension-only members need a complete path for opposite load directions. Attachment capacity, slack and eccentricity remain critical; one cable is not a universal replacement |
| 5 | A solid thicker/wider leg and enlarged connection region | More through-bolt wood bearing can help where thin 2x12s could not | Heavier; retained 4x6 rim may still control. Repeating a wider-leg-only change is lower value than fixing the entire joint |

If simplicity is the aim, investigate the continuous knee first. If a clean
side silhouette is the aim, the plywood gusset is the more promising distinct
concept. A floor triangle is plausible but changes more geometry and adds stock.
No new calculations or candidate geometry are commissioned by this ideation note.

Connection behavior, eccentricity and moisture movement are established timber
detailing concerns; see [APA connection guidance](https://www.apawood.org/guides-tools-training/technical-document-library/technical-guides/glulam-connection-details/)
and [USDA Wood Handbook, Fastenings](https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/62253).
These references support the principles, not a capacity claim for these sketches.

## Weight priorities

The current mesh estimate is about 209.45 kg: 197.74 kg wood/plywood and 11.71 kg
steel, using assumed densities. It includes modeled T-nuts but excludes holds,
hold bolts, unmodeled retention screws, lighting and wiring. These are estimates,
not measured shipping or installed weights.

1. **Simplify the brace overlap.** The four knee blanks contain about 7.02 kg of
   wood before machining. Removing their duplicated 269.51 mm overlap could save
   about 1.72 kg of gross wood plus eight splice bolt stacks, before any new
   attachment hardware. This has the clearest connection to a useful simplification.
2. **Selectively reduce legs while keeping effective braces.** Both present leg
   blanks total about 30.23 kg. Smaller sections could offer meaningful savings,
   but must retain bolt edge distances, bearing thickness and both-axis stability.
   As a scale example only, 4x4 blanks would save about 11 kg but reduce in-plane
   section modulus by roughly 60 percent; they also compromise current half-inch
   bolt edge space. This is not an endorsed substitution or a predicted saving.
3. **Trim only genuinely unused material and choose fitting bolt lengths.** The
   completed exterior trims already saved 1.41 kg. Do not count it again or remove
   the retained 18 mm leg-top reserve. Hardware savings are limited and usable
   shank, nut seating and full thread engagement still control.
4. **Leave face plywood and panel receivers alone for now.** Thinner panels,
   missing rails or large lightening cutouts would reopen the accepted panel
   construction and can change load distribution. Those are deeper redesigns,
   not demonstrated weight reductions without compromise.

A lower analysis ratio is not a proportional allowance to remove wood. A reduced
section changes stiffness, fastener forces and stability together. The practical
order is brace simplification, then selective leg sizing if enough mass benefit
remains. No further weight reduction is presently established as harmless.
