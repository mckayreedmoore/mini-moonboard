# Backing retention with relaxed housing contact

This extends the [projected two-support calculation](wide-backing-leverage.md)
to balance both bending moments generated by an outward load at each actual
panel attachment. It is **not contact FEA or a prediction of physical bolt forces**.

## Defined isolated model

The two bolt axes provide inward, nonnegative normal forces at their actual
X/S coordinates. The two housing backs provide outward compression over their
full rectangular envelopes: X −127 to −38.1 mm and 0 to 88.9 mm, each spanning
S 0 to 88.9 mm. Retention and compression balance a unit outward load at the
actual attachment X/S location. These parallel normal forces balance normal
force and moments about X and S; the other three wrench components are zero
under this specifically normal-only loading.

The calculation minimizes **total bolt-axis retention**, not the largest bolt
force. A nonnegative force at each rectangle corner can represent the force and
first moments of any nonnegative pressure distribution inside that rectangle.
The optimization permits unlimited, concentrated pressure and ignores openings,
deformation compatibility and stiffness. The complete rectangles include material
removed by machining. This enlarged admissible set is deliberately optimistic.
The resulting minimum is a lower bound **only within this isolated normal-only
model**, not for the entire board: panel/frame redistribution, side restraints,
friction and other force directions are absent.

Each saved solution includes a feasible reaction allocation and an independent
dual certificate. Nonnegative forces, equilibrium, dual inequalities and equal
primal/dual objectives are checked. That certifies the small optimization
problem, not the physical suitability of its assumptions. The optimizer's
reaction allocation is not a uniquely predicted bolt-sharing arrangement.

## Results per newton of outward attachment load

| Attachment suffix | Minimum total retention, N/N |
| --- | ---: |
| lower_left_3 | 1.500 |
| lower_left_9 | 5.133 |
| lower_left_10 | 3.132 |
| lower_right_1 | 1.500 |
| lower_right_9 | 3.148 |
| lower_right_10 | 5.462 |

For the rightmost attachment, allowing these idealized contact rectangles lowers
the minimum total retention from the earlier projected model's 7.024 N/N to
5.462 N/N. This comparison also restores the real S offset; it is not an FEA
mesh-convergence result. The two attachments directly above bolt X coordinates
still need 1.5 N/N in this model because they act at S 60 mm while the bolts
are at S 40 mm. Contact must supply the balancing moment.

Finite housing extent therefore does not remove the central-retention leverage
concern in the isolated model, even with these favorable pressure assumptions.
Do not scale a climber's weight by these coefficients: the actual load delivered
to each panel attachment is still unknown. No washer, bolt, insert, housing or
rail strength check has passed as a result of this calculation.

## Next decision

Investigate retaining the backing closer to its outer panel attachments, while
preserving access and removability, alongside the current housing/bolt detail.
The intended comparison must include realistic panel-to-frame transfer and
connection resistance. Do not increase central stock again solely to improve
the ideal-bonded frame's displacement. No geometry is changed by this study.
