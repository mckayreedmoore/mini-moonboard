# Assembled response of the 277 mm shoe-free candidate

The internal-force calculation is now implemented and has produced converged
whole-frame results. The main finding is a **leg-connection issue rather than a
reason to enlarge every 2×6 member**. In the evaluated upper-left loading case,
the leg's gross-section check is below its reference limit, while the existing
four-bolt connection exceeds its conditional lateral reference. This is a
calculated design shortfall under stated assumptions, not a predicted collapse
weight.

This report concerns the published `no-shoes-development` geometry at commit
`e546017`, with the 277 mm kicker datum. A separate agent has simplified its
source dependencies and added a current-candidate rebuild check. The numerical
evidence retains its original source snapshots; it must not be relabeled as
having been generated by the refactored sources. A fresh preparation after the refactor produced a **byte-identical initial
solver input deck**, with 24,342 nodes and 4,912 elements. The
[equivalence record](../fea/results/current-frame-response-equivalence.json)
authenticates that comparison. It confirms the same analyzed members,
connections, materials and loads; it does not qualify details omitted from
the response model.

## What was calculated

The model includes all 18 timber members, six independent plywood panels,
66 panel/kicker screws, eight leg bolts, and 24 ML24Z angles with their 144 SDS
screws. It includes timber-to-timber bearing, panel seating, and six timber feet
that can lift off. The feet are assumed not to slide. The pad and kicker plywood
receive no floor-support credit.

The modeled mass is 171.039 kg. A separate 25 kg equipment allowance is applied
at the centered upper hold location. Each climbing case acts through a 20 mm
panel patch with a resultant 100 mm in front of the climbing face. A compatible
nodal couple preserves that force's eccentricity. This represents an applied
load resultant; it is not a detailed hold-seat pressure model.

The timber uses the NDS Douglas Fir–Larch No. 2 modulus with published FPL
orthotropic ratios. Four bonded solid layers reproduce the APA reference
panel's separate axial and bending stiffnesses. The mathematical layers do not
claim to reproduce the purchased panel's veneer schedule. Individual fastener
springs distinguish withdrawal from lateral slip; joint rotation and load
sharing emerge from their positions and member deformation.

The [material and stiffness basis](current-response-material-basis.md) identifies
published properties and engineering analogies. The
[resistance audit](current-response-resistance-basis.md) separates current bolt
geometry from inherited conservative assumptions. All strength comparisons use
normal-duration factors of 1.0; a doubled downward load is a project sensitivity,
not a measured impact spectrum.

The 250 lb doubled-load case also raises the left rim’s necessary gross-section
ratio to **1.271**. The central F10 case raises one panel screw head-bearing
comparison to **2.100**. Those results prevent a blanket statement that all
members and panel attachments are adequate.

For the same A12/150 doubled-load case, scaling all mechanical fastener
stiffnesses to half and twice the reference changes maximum timber displacement
to **18.70 mm** and **11.08 mm**, respectively. The governing conditional
nominal-diameter bolt ratios remain above unity: **1.653** and **2.185**.
This sensitivity supports prioritizing the joint, but it does not turn the
assumed stiffness range into a measured bound.

The calculation therefore answers the narrow 150 lb question conditionally:
static loading is below the listed leg-bolt reference, while the doubled-load
plus rearward-force case is not. Neither observation establishes a safe climber
weight limit; even the static case has an outstanding panel-screw comparison.

The refined upper-left case also resolves the connection moment. About the
centroid of its four bolts, the load transferred to the left leg is
`(−38.5, +513.1, −2031.3) N`, with moment
`(−219.9, +14.7, +6.9) N·m` in global X/Y/Z coordinates. Thus the joint transfers
approximately **0.220 kN·m about X**, as well as the leg force; dividing total
vertical load equally among four bolts would miss that demand.

At member sections, the left leg’s maximum absolute axial force is 2.150 kN,
and its maximum strong/weak bending moments are 0.218/0.0548 kN·m. The left
rim’s corresponding values are 0.850 kN and 0.798/0.0263 kN·m. These component
maxima can occur at different section cuts; the reported interaction checks
use simultaneous actions at each cut, not an invented combination of maxima.

A ratio above 1.0 exceeds the stated analytical reference. A ratio below 1.0
passes only that listed comparison. It does not establish an overall climber
rating or address omitted connection and stability conditions.

## Numerical verification

Four native solver patch checks reproduce both panel directions' axial and
bending stiffnesses within 0.000016%. They verify the implemented material axes,
layer connectivity and section stiffness; they are not new physical plywood
tests.

The coarse and refined upper-left runs preserve the load patch, joints,
materials and support rules. Refinement changes the governing leg-bolt demand
by approximately 0.04%, maximum timber displacement by 0.32%, and maximum panel
midsurface displacement by 3.79%. The bolt conclusion is insensitive to that
refinement. These checks do not demonstrate convergence of every local stress
near a hole or screw head.

Every published case must pass global force/moment balance, member equilibrium,
constraint interpolation, and compression-only contact checks. Member force
recovery propagates the actual printed displacement precision through the spring
forces. The report retains raw residuals and their rounding intervals; it does
not disguise a failed equilibrium check by simply increasing a tolerance.

Two exploratory cases were rerun from an isolated source snapshot after the
concurrent refactor began. Their earlier mixed-source runs are excluded from
the published comparison.

## Engineering disposition

- **Retain the single-2×6 baseline.** The evaluated leg section is not the reason
  to select larger lumber. Full-length unbraced member checks are reported as
  sensitivities; panel-connected framing is not automatically assumed unbraced.
- **Resolve the leg joint before release.** Its actual calculated force and
  moment provide a concrete design target. Increasing bolt steel grade alone
  does not improve the governing wood-bearing yield mode. A revised bolt-group
  layout or a direct timber-bearing detail should be evaluated in the assembled
  model before selecting larger stock or custom steel.
- **Keep the accepted plywood and T-nuts.** Some individual screw comparisons
  exceed their conservative references. That identifies attachment load
  transfer as a follow-up, not a demonstrated need for different plywood or
  T-nuts. The present model omits clamp friction and nonlinear redistribution;
  those effects must not be credited without a defensible basis.
- **Retain the base-angle applicability question precisely.** Listed directional
  force components remain separate from unlisted separation and flange moments.
  An unlisted direction is not a zero-capacity rating. The report supplies the
  actual force and moment for resolving that installation detail.

The next design action is therefore a targeted connection revision, checked
against these assembled-frame demands. There is no value in searching every
remaining hold position to erase a shortfall already found in an evaluated
specified case. After a viable connection detail is established, complete the
load-case envelope, applicable member stability/local-opening and serviceability
checks, and the matching construction package. No physical floor-friction test
or general plywood/T-nut testing campaign is added.

## Reproduction and evidence

The analysis programs are independent of the viewer and do not regenerate
historical STEP/STL assets. Use a stable checkout or the stored source snapshots
when another agent is editing the geometry dependencies.

```python
from fea.current_response_run import run

run("fea/generated/my-current-response", hold="A12", pounds=150.,
    dynamic_factor=2., horizontal_force=(0., 300.),
    frame_size=150., panel_size=120., patch_size=20.)
```

Set `dynamic_factor=1` and `horizontal_force=(0, 0)` for the static comparison.
The explicit `connection_scale=0.5` and `connection_scale=2` cases are model
sensitivities, not manufacturer confidence bounds. Running the code against
later sources creates new evidence; it does not reproduce the pinned result
merely because the candidate name is unchanged.

uv run python -m fea.current_response_coupon --output fea/generated/current-panel-coupon
