# Wood-first frame redesign

## Accepted direction

The next candidate must not require custom metal fabrication. The owner does
not have equipment for making steel brackets. The previous inspection model
contains 30 custom steel connectors and 120 associated fasteners; replacing
only its two extended top brackets would not satisfy this requirement.

Use the supplied commercial-frame photograph as a functional reference, not
as a manufacturing template. Investigate two continuous central supports,
horizontal rails at the panel midpoint and kicker transition, and direct
wood-to-wood bearing into the perimeter frame. Housed joints, conventional
mortise-and-tenon joints, and accessible mechanical retention are candidates.
Do not presume that removing brackets means removing the restraint they supplied.

Develop dimensions and connections from this project's purchased stock,
official panel/grid interface, hardware clearances and load path. Do not trace
the reference's profiles, infer hidden joints as facts, or preserve the old
steel-hole patterns merely to reuse a fastener schedule. Additional panel
support may be necessary; the photo does not establish unsupported panel spans
or connection capacity.

Ordinary wood cutting, routing/chiseling and drilling are permitted. Purchased
metal connectors are optional where their unmodified factory geometry and
specified fasteners fit; no cutting, drilling, bending or welding steel is
required from the owner. Old custom-steel variants remain historical inspection
models, not the recommended build route under this new constraint.

## Practical checks

- Preserve the purchased face material, official hold/LED axes, climbing-side
  orientation, independent leg plies and level feet.
- Check remaining wood around housings and tenons, actual bearing surfaces,
  mechanical retention, assembly access and screw/bolt interference.
- Do not replace the old steel connections with unexamined end-grain screws.
- Redesign the short-margin kicker splice rather than retaining its old holes.
- Keep the pad separate and retain the no-anchoring/no-assumed-ballast limits.
- Publish original CAD and drawings, updated metric/imperial schedules and
  explicit structural-review gates. Website text remains professional prose.

## Reference use and intellectual-property limits

For a US-oriented preliminary assessment, copyright distinguishes functional
ideas/methods from their particular expression. The Copyright Office explains
that [ideas, methods and systems are not protected by copyright](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ31.pdf).
That is a reason to develop original drawings and geometry from functional
requirements, not permission to republish another maker's plans, CAD or artwork.

Patents are a separate question: the USPTO describes rights over
[making and using patented inventions](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/manage),
and [design patents concern ornamental appearance](https://www.uspto.gov/patents/basics/apply/design-patent).
Independent development or cosmetic differences do not by themselves establish
patent clearance. There is no assumed percentage-change safe harbor.

The owner identifies the pictured wooden frame as a Moon Climbing product
available by telephone but not yet publicly listed. No relevant-rights search
or freedom-to-operate opinion is complete,
and no statement here guarantees freedom from IP claims. Keep third-party
branding/artwork separate from original frame plans and retain the project's
independent, non-endorsed description. Obtain appropriate legal review for
clearance before commercial distribution or sale; private construction is not
assumed to create a general patent exemption.
