# Outer-header rim-first sequence: conditional only

The [bounded dependency probe][probe] finds no fastener-cycle preventing the side rim
from being removed before operating a *recessed* outer-header/post barrel bolt.
That outer-header duty joins the header to the outer post, not to the side rim.
The two fixed fastener inventories retain their original axes: 66 panel/kicker
screws and 12 frame bolts. **Preserving those axes does not mean leaving every
fastener installed during disassembly.** If the rim screws and leg bolts must
stay installed, rim-first service is blocked.

For each rim, the source inventory identifies eight main-panel screws, two
original leg/rim frame bolts, and five rim-touching candidate barrel stations:
top-outer, bottom-outer, lower-outer, upper-outer and base-outer/side. Each
candidate station currently depicts two barrel-bolt rows, so ten trial bolts
per rim would also need to be undone. The outer-header/post bolt can remain
installed while these are released. In reverse assembly, the header/post bolt
could be operated while the rim is absent, followed by rim installation and
reconnection on the original fixed axes. The board and panels would have to be
unloaded and independently supported throughout.

With all other *uncut wood* left in place, the probe samples each bare rim at
0–160 mm in 5 mm increments along the rearward board normal. The last sample
exceeds the rim's nominal 139.7 mm normal depth. Neither rim has a
positive-volume overlap above 1 mm³ at those sample positions. This is not a
continuous swept-volume proof. It omits installed hardware, hold/T-nut
projections, electrical parts, handling clearance, tolerances and tool motions.
In particular, the ten rim-joint trial bolts per side have not been shown
individually accessible for removal while the rest of the frame is assembled.
The outer-header recessed head, seat and driver path are separate geometry
questions; this sequence only removes the *rim-specific* access obstacle.

Result: **topologically possible, operationally unverified**. It is a useful
candidate order, not a repeatable-assembly, drilling, fabrication, strength or
climbing release. Do not use the sampled path as removal instructions.
