# Current crash-pad construction record

The owner built two portable 48 × 72 × 5-inch bouldering pads. They sit
side by side below the board, producing a 96 × 72-inch covered footprint with
the center seam running front to back.

Each owner-reported nominal stack, top to bottom, is:

- two 0.5-inch layers of black 1.7 lb/ft³ nominal-density polyethylene foam;
- one 3-inch 44-ILD polyurethane foam core; and
- two 0.5-inch layers of the same polyethylene foam.

The purchased polyurethane stock was two 36 × 96 × 3-inch pieces, Amazon
ASIN B071LMCHX3. The purchased polyethylene stock was eight 48 × 108 ×
0.5-inch, 1.7 lb/ft³ nominal-density sheets from ADR Packaging in Utah, four
sheets per finished pad. The
polyethylene sheets were trimmed for the finished footprint. The polyurethane
stock has the same plan area as the two finished 48 × 72-inch cores, but this
record does not invent an unreported internal cut or joint layout.

The owner followed the
[Crag Crafts DIY highball-pad video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_MYbZUr4orU).
The [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B071LMCHX3) identifies the
purchased core item; the [GoTo Foam catalog](https://www.gotofoam.com/product-category/96-inch-length/)
describes a 3 × 36 × 96-inch 44-ILD foam product. The
[ADR Packaging foam page](https://adrpackaging.com/foam-sheets/) corroborates
black 1.7-pound polyethylene sheet material, though the exact 48 × 108-inch
purchased size is recorded from the owner rather than the current page.

The viewer represents only the finished rectangular footprint and nominal
five-inch stack thickness. It does not model cover construction, hinge behavior,
internal foam joints, handles or closures. No impact attenuation, critical fall
height, seam performance or certification is claimed.
