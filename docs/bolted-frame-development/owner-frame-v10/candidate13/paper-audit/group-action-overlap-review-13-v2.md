## Does the diagnostic reduction count load sharing twice?

There is a real potential overlap, but no demonstrated permission to remove a factor in this design.

For a deliberately restricted scalar example, let a parallel, same-sign group have identical per-bolt capacity Z, total force P and exact fractions alpha_i summing to1. The direct individual check is max(alpha_i)P/Z. Define the equivalent distribution factor Cg_dist=1/[n max(alpha_i)]. Then the aggregate check P/[n Z Cg_dist] equals the direct individual check exactly. Dividing the already resolved individual index by that same factor again repeats the same distribution penalty. For two bolts with fractions0.8/0.2, Cg_dist=0.625; both correct expressions give0.8P/Z, while the repeated operation gives1.28P/Z. This is a mathematical illustration, not a selected candidate13 factor.

Primary engineering literature explains the NDS distribution mechanism in terms of member extensional stiffness, fastener count/spacing and single-fastener load-slip behavior. It also distinguishes that elastic distribution model from wood limit states and localized crushing. See Rammer and Line, *Development of Failure Mechanisms for Fasteners in the United States*, sections2.1.1–2.2, [USDA Forest Products Laboratory](https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/27033.pdf). This historical mechanism reference does not replace2024NDS provisions.

Candidate13 resolves unequal sharing from gross timber/angle/shaft compliance, gaps, contact engagement, eccentricity and moment. It does not calibrate orthotropic embedment force-slip, wood crushing/plastic redistribution or splitting, and its beam interpolation is not a verified reproduction of each NDS row-segment EA. Its multidirectional forces, end captures and local couples also violate the simple scalar illustration. Therefore it is unjustified both to declare that every trial1/n is required by NDS and to declare Cg=1 because individual forces are unequal. The trial remains visible until replaced transparently by a source-supported complete criterion.
