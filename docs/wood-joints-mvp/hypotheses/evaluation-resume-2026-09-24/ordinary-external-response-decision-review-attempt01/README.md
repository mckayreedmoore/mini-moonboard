# WJ24 ordinary-joint external-response decision review

Date: 2026-09-27  
Scope: the owner-reviewed `led-clearance-2x6-runner-seated-blocks-v1` bottom-center-right ordinary patch. This is a method/status decision record, not a response result or criterion disposition.

## Disposition

The original runnable native event model was not recovered. The source-bound replacement is reconstructable from the preserved mesh and producer artifacts, and its geometry, external-port maps, equations, contact coverage, and load maps have independent input audits. That evidence supports bounded diagnostics; it does not demonstrate a mechanical response. The instrumented event run's exact deck, executable, complete input bundle, and raw accepted-state files are absent. Its hashes and compact event reports are not substitutes for those files.

No accepted external rail/principal force–motion response exists. The 12 signed external-port force decks remain input-only, and the preflight marks `response_ready: false`. The zero-load reference-tangent witness is a specific cleat-only translation along local `N`, with scaled residual `1.375e-12`; it does not prove total nullity or a physical failure. This mode has zero projection into the two external-port coordinates. Prescribing an internal cleat/principal seating coordinate directly drives that mode and adds an actuator path; fixing the cleat or adding stabilization would invent support and is excluded.

The accepted state-39/40 transient is not the requested external-member response. At state 39, its applied loads act on the cleat and principal (equal-and-opposite member resultants; load-pattern factor `0.099291`), so the case directly drives the cleat mode. It reports first bore contact and one following accepted state, but the retained records omit the full external-port displacement projection and cleat-node displacement needed for port `q` and cleat pose. The raw `.dat` and `.frd` files are not retained. Its contact and wrench audits remain a bounded event diagnostic.

The compact per-body wrench report also lists only known applied-load, contact, inertia, and authored-fit terms; it excludes continuum internal-force transfer and solver-generated nut-carrier constraint reactions. It is not a closed per-body equilibrium. In the prepared port-motion input, the force dual map uses all 43 nodes of each source-bound cap, while the motion fit excludes contact-surface nodes and uses 25 nodes, renormalizing the remaining weights. An independent parent reconstruction confirms that each omitted set carries `0.3563359` of the cap's original area weight; both maps reproduce a rigid section to about `3e-15`, but they are not the same functional for arbitrary flexible-cap displacement. The two coordinates are therefore not exact work-conjugates for a flexible cap. The frozen n+ controller also uses 0.01 mm effective endpoint in attempt08. Before comparing force and motion results, use a common full-cap map or independently calculate canonical full-cap `q` and quantify its difference from the controlled coordinate. MPC control-node `RF` is not a generalized reaction.

The parent rebuilt this coordinate path in [attempt 09](ordinary-port-motion-attempt09-common-map/README.md). Its equations use the full 43-node force-dual map, choose dependent DOFs away from contact and nut constraints, retain contact nodes as independent variables, add source-bound `SOF` output on both sections, and monitor all 9,369 cleat nodes. The independent audit confirms exact map agreement, maximum serialized-elimination error `3.36e-12`, random virtual-work error `3.87e-16`, and no contact/nut dependent-DOF collision. The first external `n_plus` static run then spent 901 seconds and 33 Newton iterations on its first 0.001 mm increment without accepting a state; its declared parent timeout stopped it, not an OOM. No `.dat` or `.sta` response was produced. Thus the map/output defect is repaired, while the free-cleat static contact equilibrium remains the blocker.

## Assumptions that may be frozen for a diagnostic

Keep the exact reviewed geometry and contact graph; the cleat remains free. Use the nominal centered CAD configuration with the modeled 7.5 mm wood bore and 6.35 mm shaft, which implies 0.575 mm radial travel as a geometry scenario. Preserve openable, compression-only, frictionless wood and bore contact, zero preload, and no gap adjustment. Use the documented DF-L No. 2 longitudinal-modulus plus clear-wood-ratio orthotropic tensor as a named elastic scenario, with radial/tangential orientation swapped where ring orientation is unknown. Treat the ideal stiff nut/shaft fit as one named engagement scenario only.

These choices are transparent analysis branches, not measured hardware/material facts and not globally conservative bounds. Zero friction and zero preload remove credited mechanisms but can shift force sharing either way. The stiff engagement case can overstate axial transfer. The model has no validated timber crushing/splitting, bolt yielding/bending, washer bending, thread stripping, or withdrawal law; its elastic stresses and contact forces are not capacities. Do not infer equal bolt sharing from geometry or pressure from nominal face area.

## Next bounded decision after static pilot

**Decision:** Can a physically loaded external rail/principal path develop a stable post-clearance response while the cleat remains free?

**Observation:** From one source-frozen replacement model, retain accepted-state six-DOF motion at both external ports, their work-conjugate wrench at common datums, the free cleat's six-DOF pose, all four bolt/bore resultants, all three timber-face resultants and gaps, and force/moment/work accounting. The load must enter through the rail and principal ports, not directly through the cleat. Recover port wrench with audited section resultants (for example, a verified CalculiX surface-section print) or another independently checked method, not MPC control-node `RF`. Ensure the force map and reported motion use the same full-cap projection. Stop at first actual engagement plus a predeclared short post-contact sequence, or on a source/numerical defect. Distinguish a transient path from static stiffness and include inertia in any dynamic balance.

**Next step:** Test one separate source-bound transient driven only through the external rail/principal ports, with the cleat still free. Use the declared density scenario and report kinetic energy relative to elastic plus contact energy. Call the result a quasi-static characterization only if that ratio and a ramp-rate comparison meet the recorded gates; otherwise retain a dynamic-only diagnostic and leave static response unresolved. If a stable response branch is obtained, freeze matched timestep, local mesh, contact-penalty, grain-frame, clearance, and engagement comparisons at the same response states, then report a state/history-dependent response relation over the tested range.

**Clearance correction:** The attempt 09 pre-run rationale used 0.575 mm as if it were the full relative port travel. Under its symmetric controls, each port moves half the relative coordinate. For a centered free cleat, the two-sided idealized bore travel is about 1.15 mm before both rows are forced into bearing; actual onset must be determined from local bore and face contact output. The 1.0 mm static pilot never accepted its first 0.001 mm state, so it did not test engagement. A next diagnostic may target roughly 1.3 mm relative N travel to pass that idealized travel modestly, but this remains a geometry scenario, not a demand or conservative bound. Transient force and moment accounting must include inertia and momentum, and energy accounting must include kinetic energy; do not apply static equal-and-opposite closure as if the moving bodies had zero inertia.

Attempt 09 records analyst-selected numerical-only force, moment, work and comparison bounds tied to its unit-wrench scale. They are not engineering acceptance tolerances. The static run used one serialized source-bound input set, pinned executable, 900-second parent timeout and post-exit hashing. Do not fix the cleat, close gaps, add friction/preload, or tune damping to force a pass. Repeated amplitude/time variants without a changed physical question are not justified.

## Senior design-engineering route and normal practice

For the response question, use a piecewise, state-dependent multi-port relation: external-member motions and wrenches plus cleat pose, bolt actions, face resultants, contact/opening state, and history. A single zero-load linear stiffness matrix is not an appropriate representation across open clearance and contact engagement.

In parallel, use the existing finished-geometry screen to prepare conditional detailing and resistance calculations. The 2024 NDS route can check applicable single-bolt yield modes and, separately, spacing/end/edge, group action, net section, bearing, and other brittle modes when actual products, signed demands, and property bases are supplied. This does not supply joint stiffness or the load share among the seat and four bolts. ASTM D5652 describes single-bolt connection testing and states that full combination performance is established by testing the complete assembly; a matched complete-joint test or an applicable validated response model is the usual way to support custom slip/load-sharing behavior.

The detailed 3D contact model is reasonable as a bounded diagnostic for this unusual multi-path joint. Published timber-connection FE work validates its load–slip predictions against tests; it does not treat convergence alone as validation. Thus free-mode checks, contact diagnostics, source reconstruction, and a small sensitivity study are normal specialist analysis tasks. The prolonged custom solver/output-debug loop is not the ordinary first-line design route and should be time-boxed around the external-response decision. Code/component checks, tests, or a simpler calibrated component model are common alternatives; none may inherit this model's response without demonstrating applicability.
