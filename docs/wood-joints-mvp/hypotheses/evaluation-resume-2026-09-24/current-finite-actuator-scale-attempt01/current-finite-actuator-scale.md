# Current-joint finite-actuator scale estimate

For a first controlled q-displacement diagnostic, use a **200 N/mm numerical actuator spring**, a **0.1 s C² quintic target ramp** to **1.15 mm**, and a **0.5 ms maximum increment** as the initial scale to assess. These values follow a two-member rigid-translation inertia estimate from the frozen current mesh; they are not physical joint stiffness or a validated timestep.

The pinned unit-load helper defines q as the fitted bottom-cleat displacement along `N = (0, -0.7660444431, 0.6427876097)` minus the fitted principal displacement along N at the common source datum. Its one-newton nodal pattern balances globally to about `9e-16 N` and `5e-13 N·mm`. The mesh gives integrated masses of `0.0005581872214 tonne` (0.5582 kg) for the cleat and `0.007838834439 tonne` (7.8388 kg) for the principal. Treating those as two freely translating rigid members gives the relative-coordinate reduced mass

`μ = (m_c m_p)/(m_c + m_p) = 0.0005210820445 tonne = 0.521082 kg`.

This translation-only reduction omits rotations and the rail, bolts, and washers. The frozen mesh metadata has centroids and volumes but no inertia tensors, so the calculation makes no rotational-mass claim. The separate native mass audit confirms projected translation masses for the cleat and rail and bolt unions, but does not provide the cleat/principal rigid-body inertia tensor at the q datum. The archived mass matrix is pinned, but this note does not project it into second moments.

At 200 N/mm, this two-member reduction gives `ω = √(Ka/μ) = 619.5 rad/s`, `f = 98.6 Hz`, and a free q period of `10.14 ms`. A 0.1 s quintic target ramp spans about 9.9 periods. Its maximum target acceleration is `663.95 mm/s²`; the inertial force scale is `0.346 N`, corresponding to a slow-ramp tracking-lag scale of `0.00173 mm` at this Ka. A 0.5 ms increment resolves the isolated actuator period with about 20 increments. The 100 N/mm comparison point gives about 69.7 Hz, a 14.34 ms period, and a `0.0034597 mm` tracking-lag scale at the same ramp. The two points form a controller sensitivity range, not a measured material or joint property.

Use the quintic target `q_target = 1.15(10s³ − 15s⁴ + 6s⁵) mm`, with `s = clamp(t / 0.1 s, 0, 1)`. Record the fitted physical q separately from the target and the massless proxy coordinate. Also record actuator force `Ka(q_target − q_actual)`, controller spring energy `½Ka(q_target − q_actual)²`, and target work separately from contact penalty energy, internal strain energy, and kinetic energy. The controller is a diagnostic driver, not a physical connector. Do not infer the contact reaction from a proxy-node RF alone.

The estimate does not bound frequencies of flexible wood, bolt, washer, contact, or rigid nut-envelope modes. Contacts, open clearances, and modelled nut coupling can change the effective q inertia and stiffness. `k1e4` in the cited seating pilot names a contact penalty in N/mm³; it is unrelated to this actuator stiffness in N/mm. That pilot's CLOAD response is inertia-dominated and is not used as a static stiffness calibration. Physical nut bores and thread engagement remain unverified, and no complete-joint response or acceptance claim follows.
